What is this?

Cultural Borders is an interactive map that shows what people are actually listening to across different countries, regions, and cities — pulled directly from YouTube Music Charts. Tap any territory on the map and you get the local top charts for that place.

The idea came from The Pudding's Cultural Borders piece, which published a one-time snapshot showing where the world's musical tastes diverge. This version is built to stay current and let you explore more than just the #1 song.

What can you explore?

The map has four modes, switchable from the bar at the bottom:

  • Tracks Map coloured by the #1 song in each territory. Tap to see the full local top 20.
  • Artists Same idea but for artists — who dominates each place.
  • Discover One song per territory picked for being locally unique — something that barely charts anywhere else in the world. Tap a country and it plays immediately.
  • Languages Map coloured by the dominant language in each territory's top 20. Tap to see the full language breakdown with percentages.

Where does the data come from?

YouTube Music exposes chart data for around 4,700 locations worldwide — countries, regions, and cities. A Python script queries those charts in parallel and stores the top 20 tracks and artists per location.

The map geometry comes from OpenStreetMap via Nominatim. Each territory's polygon is flattened so higher-detail areas (cities, regions) punch holes into country-level shapes, giving a seamless non-overlapping globe.

Who made it?
